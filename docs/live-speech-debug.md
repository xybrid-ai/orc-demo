# Live speech debugging

In `GoblinSpeakingDemo`, F5 jumps straight to free-form input, without synthesizing a welcome or starting idle taunts during this debug test. F4 shows or hides the runtime debug panel. Its editable handoff defaults to Mira, selling turnips, a bribe attempt, passage granted, and patience 3. Change the fields and press **Jump / apply summary** to start a fresh turn with those values.

The panel keeps each delivered line's source (authored, LLM, LLM fallback, placeholder), raw model output, parsed emotion/intensity, requested patience delta and resulting patience, model time and total voice synthesis time. It shows RTF as synthesis seconds divided by generated audio seconds; below 1 means faster than playback. The model timer includes any wait for preload and the serial model worker. The voice timer sums synthesis across a line (all of its clauses for Kitten) and excludes Rhubarb.

Every delivered voice line is freshly synthesized. Authored text and fallback text use the same live voice path. There is no waveform reuse, prerecorded reaction, or debug-file playback. Kitten's matched WarChief files, rc.4 bridge, Accelerate, and unlimited token setting are retained.

Phonon speaks each line as one utterance (`phononWholeLines`, on by default). It renders about 35 times faster than playback, so nothing is gained by starting on a first clause, and one utterance keeps its intonation across commas and sentences. Phonon pads every call with about 150 ms of silence in front and 0.6–1 s behind (a bare "Hmph." runs to its frame cap); played clause by clause, each comma and full stop used to become a gap of about a second. `GatekeeperSpeechTrim` measures silence against the loudest 10 ms window of the line (Phonon's floor sits near −66 dB, word endings decay through −40 dB), keeps 25 ms before the first sound above −50 dB and 100 ms after the last, fades both edges, and shortens any internal pause longer than 400 ms to 400 ms. The line then plays as one complete clip; it never plays from a partially generated waveform. Measured on an M5 Max (one scripted encounter, 26 lines, compared with the clause-by-clause recordings of the previous Play session): silence after a line fell from a median 630 ms to 90 ms, pauses inside a line from a median 710 ms to at most 400 ms (commas about 260 ms), lines got 25–35% shorter, and whisper large-v3-turbo heard 2.4% word errors instead of 3.9%, with no last word missed in either. The first sound of a line comes about 100–150 ms later, because the whole line is synthesized before it plays (90–220 ms).

**Speech text.** Phonon (Pocket TTS lineage, trained on single sentences) treats every `.` as a sentence end even inside one utterance: the voice falls, pauses and starts again. So for a whole Phonon line the voice is given `GatekeeperSpeechText.Flow(text)` while the subtitle keeps the authored text. Internal full stops become commas (not after an initial or a short abbreviation), quotes are dropped, ellipses and dashes become commas, `?` and `!` stay (a mid-line `?` rises) and the line keeps one closing mark. A common word after a former stop is lowercased; a name keeps its capital. On Trola's clone this cut the raw gap at a former stop from 660 to 355 ms and the pitch reset from 7.7 to 5.9 semitones, with fewer transcription errors (one experiment, three seeds; `tools/npc-quality/speech-text-cases.json` pins the rules). The F4 button **Speech text: flowing / as written** switches Trola and Vesper together from the next line and is remembered (`-xybridSpeechText written|flowing` overrides it for a run); Restart replays a route for comparison. Kitten clauses and the warm-up are always spoken as written. The panel and F3 show **Spoken as:** when the words differ.

Kitten (and Kokoro) still split text into clauses at commas, semicolons, colons, sentence punctuation and dashes, because Kitten is slower than playback. A complete nonstreaming PCM clip is generated for each clause. The first starts playing while the voice worker prepares subsequent clauses. If the next is still being generated, playback waits at the clause boundary. A clause never plays from a partially generated waveform; its AudioSource plays the whole clip before the next starts.

Each fully generated line is saved as played (trimmed, for Phonon) as a 24 kHz mono PCM16 WAV with a text sidecar (the displayed text on the first line, then `speech:` and `style:` lines) under `Application.persistentDataPath/TrolaSpeechDebug`. On this Mac that is `~/Library/Application Support/DefaultCompany/Orc Demo/TrolaSpeechDebug`. These are diagnostic output only. Rhubarb receives the line or clause text through `--dialogFile` and a separate 120 Hz high-pass, peak-normalized waveform with the same number of samples. Playback uses the original. Its cues attach to their matching clip; with whole lines, Rhubarb starts once the whole line is synthesized and analyzes all of it, so a line plays on the loudness jaw a little longer before its cues arrive (Rhubarb spends most of its run starting up); the loudness jaw covers pending or failed analysis, with a 120 Hz high-pass, an adaptive rumble floor, a raised relative noise threshold and smoothed attack/release.

The installed Xybrid 0.9.0 loader rejects Kitten's `ZzzEmbed` execution template. The direct Kitten rc.4 ABI is CPU-only, uses greedy token decoding, and exposes no stable/expressive preset or Metal selector. Performance comparisons must distinguish unsupported controls from measured results; no SDK/native upgrade is implied by this debug pass.

See `voice-performance.md` for the single-observation measurements and their limitations.

Rhubarb 1.14.0 for macOS is Intel-only. Apple Silicon requires Apple’s Rosetta 2 runtime. The initial lip analysis failed with exit 255 / Bad CPU type because Rosetta was absent; after installing Rosetta, the existing binary produced cues successfully and the live Editor confirmation passed. No Rhubarb/native rebuild or SDK upgrade was needed.

Free-form Send accepts one queued reply while the current live voice is being generated or played. The button shows **Queued**, and the reply is submitted only after the current line finishes; speech is never interrupted. A nonempty draft keeps idle taunts from starting while the player reads or thinks. The debug panel shows the conversation phase and whether Send is available, waiting or queued. Restarting, applying a new summary, or ending the encounter clears a queued reply.

The input distinguishes Thinking from Preparing voice and shows elapsed stage time. `TrolaSpeechDebug/session-state.txt` is updated during Play with the current phase, queued text, audio sample position/pause/loop state, model readiness, player summary, exchanges and raw model outputs. It is diagnostic output only. Ask **What is my name?** to test player-name recall; **What is your name?** asks for Trola.

A functional Editor check confirmed the actual Send raycast, Mira/turnip recall, and delivery of a second message queued during live speech. The second generated reply requested `ends_scene=true`, so the final state was Ended with an empty queue. Bribe recall failed in that reply (it said turnips). Returning to input after an ordinary Gemma reply was not confirmed in this check. The earlier placeholder queue check did return to input. Voice latency remains unresolved.
